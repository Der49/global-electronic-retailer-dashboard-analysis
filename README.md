# Global Electronic Retailer Dashboard Analysis

An end-to-end Sales Analysis with Excel for a fictitious global electronics retailer, including tables containing information about transactions, products, customers, stores and currency exchange rates.

---
## 📌 Business Overview

The point is to find patterns through which categories, products, stores, and countries that drives revenue and profit, how have they trended over time, and how do customer demographics and purchase behavior shape those results so that we can use Data-Driven Decision-Making in the business.

### Key Insights

- Two categories carry the business. Computers (34.6%) and Home Appliances (19.4%) make up 54% of the $55.8M revenue.
- Growth stopped in 2020. Revenue rose from $6.9M in 2016 to a peak of $18.3M in 2019, then fell 49.1% in 2020. The drop hit every country and both channels.
- Profit margin is steady at 58.6%. Every category sits between 54.7% and 61.0%, so profit depends mainly on sales volume, not pricing.
- Customers come back. 61.2% of customers bought more than once, and they placed 82.5% of all orders.
- The United States and online are the anchors. US customers bring 53.6% of revenue, and the online store brings 20.5%, with its share rising from 16.8% in 2016 to 22.3% in 2020.

---

### Recommendations

- Make volume recovery the first priority. Run win-back campaigns for existing customers, since sales haven’t returned to 2019 levels.
- Keep growing online. Use bundles and accessory upsells to lift its average order value ($2,044 vs. $2,138 in-store).
- Protect Computers and Home Appliances. Keep stock and pricing strong, and cross-sell Cameras and TV and Video, which have margins near 60%.
- Reward loyal customers. Launch a loyalty program with a VIP tier for top spenders.
- Plan around the winter peak. December to February brings 38.5% of 2016–2020 revenue, so schedule inventory, staffing and promotions ahead of it.
